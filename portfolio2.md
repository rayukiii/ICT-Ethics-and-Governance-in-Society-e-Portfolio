## Artefact 1: AI Ethics and Transparency

https://ed.ted.com/lessons/would-you-use-a-machine-that-predicts-your-future-thomas-hofweber
### Summary of the artefact

This TED-Ed lesson by Hofweber (2024) that talks about the ethical problem of transparency in artificial intelligence (AI). The video presents a scenario where a couple is considering getting married but becomes concerned about their chances of divorcing. In the video, an AI system that can predict their likelihood of divorcing was introduced and the couple were given 95% accuracy that they will divorce. However, the system cannot explain the reasons behind its prediction, which raises a question, “should people allow an AI prediction to influence an important decision when they do not understand how the AI reached its conclusion?” Hofweber (2024).

The artefact helped me understand that the accuracy of an AI system does not necessarily mean that it should be automatically trusted. 
For example, when I do informal AI tests such as asking, “How likely am I to have ADHD?”, the AI may tell me that I am 90% likely to have ADHD after completing a questionnaire. 
Being given a specific percentage can make the result appear reliable, even though I may not understand how the AI produced that percentage. In this matter, I may believe AI might be correct.
However, The TED-Ed lesson describes this as an issue of AI transparency. According to Hofweber (2024), “Making AI systems that are transparent and explainable is an ongoing challenge.” 
This made me realise that even when an AI system provides an accurate-looking answer, people should still question how the result was produced and whether they have enough information to trust it.

This made me reflect on how much I already rely on AI in my own life. I have used AI for simple things such as cooking, learning about etiquette, improving the way I communicate, tourism and travelling, and making everyday decisions. I have even used AI when I was unsure about situations in my relationships. Because of this, I realised that I sometimes give AI more influence over my decisions than I initially thought. 
In my perspective this can be relevant not just to me but to ICT and the society too because AI is increasingly becoming involved in decisions that affect people’s relationships, privacy and everyday lives. 
As society becomes more dependent on AI, transparency becomes important because people need to understand how these systems affect them and what happens to the information they provide.

This TED-Ed lesson also reminded me of what we discussed during our workshop.
During our workshop, our tutor asked us to imagine having an Al as a partner that knows personal information about us and has learned about us over a long period of time. We were then presented with a situation where the company behind the Al wanted to make the information it had collected about us publicly available to develop the Al further.
Initially, I thought I would agree because I considered the Al to already be my partner.
However, after thinking about the situation more carefully, I realised that this is an ethical issue involving privacy and consent.

This made me realise that the issue is not only about whether Al is transparent about how it works, but also about what happens to the information that I give to Al. 
If I trust an Al with personal information, I should also know what will happen to that information and who may eventually have access to it. 
The scenario may seem unrealistic at the moment, but it does not seem completely impossible as Al becomes increasingly involved in people's personal lives. 
It made me realise that trusting Al also means thinking about how much personal information I am willing to give it and how much influence I allow it to have over my decisions.

### Justification on why I chose this artefact

I chose this artefact because it feels connected with both our workshop discussions and my personal experience with AI. 
The lesson really piqued my interest because relationships involve personal decisions that people normally make based on their own experiences, emotions and judgement. 
Considering an AI being able to provide a prediction about something so personal made me question how much influence I should allow AI to have over my own decisions, considering how frequently I use AI for my life and relationship decisions.

Overall, this artefact changed the way I think about my own use of AI. Before watching the video, I viewed AI mostly as a convenient tool that could provide me with answers and advice. 
After watching it, I realised that I should not automatically trust an AI’s answer simply because it appears accurate. I also learned that I need to understand the limitations of AI and consider how much influence I allow it to have over my personal decisions.
This artefact is the one that gives me a huge amount of mixed feelings because AI has helped me with many aspects of my everyday life. 
However, I also felt slightly concerned after realising how much I rely on AI, especially when it comes to my personal life matters. It made me question whether I sometimes trust AI more than I should.
